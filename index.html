HiFun is a hierarchical framework for efficient real-world functional dexterous manipulation that decouples contact-rich hand primitive skills from arm-hand coordination to simplify exploration and improve data efficiency. Across four challenging real-world tasks, HiFun achieves over 90% success on every task and an average success rate of 95.5% with less than 1 hour of online training, while demonstrating robust adaptation under disturbances.

Abstract

Dexterous manipulation with multi-fingered hands offers the potential for human-like functional behaviors. However, learning contact-rich, high-precision skills on real hardware poses challenges due to high-dimensional exploration, the cost of collecting demonstrations, and stringent spatial and temporal requirements for arm-hand coordination. In this work, we propose HiFun, a hierarchical learning framework designed to facilitate effective and efficient real-world functional dexterous manipulation. We decouple contact-rich hand primitive skills from arm-hand coordination. By structuring learning around these hand primitives, rather than searching directly in high-dimensional joint space, we can significantly simplify the exploration space and improve learning efficiency. Specifically, we employ a single kinesthetic demonstration to establish a kinematic prior for finger motions, and utilize human-in-the-loop reinforcement learning (RL) to guide arm control and hand skill activation. Additionally, we enhance fine-grained hand-object interactions through residual RL to refine contact-aware hand skills, along with a multi-head critic to improve temporal coordination between the arm and hand. We evaluate our framework on functional dexterous manipulation tasks that require precise, contact-rich hand motions and tight arm-hand coordination. Our framework achieves high success rates with short online training times and demonstrates robust adaptation to dynamic disturbances.
